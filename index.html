Principled trust in autonomy.

We develop principled methods to quantify the integrity of autonomous systems and translate uncertainty into safer decisions with quantifiable safety guarantees.

PI: Eunjeong Hyeon University of California, Riverside

About

Safe, Human-centric, Intelligent, Future Technology

SHIFT Lab studies a fundamental problem in autonomous systems: how should a system make decisions when the information behind them is uncertain, incomplete, or unreliable? We develop principled methods to quantify uncertainty and integrity, determine when predictions can be trusted, and translate that information into safer decisions and control.

A system earns trust not by acting confidently, but by knowing precisely when it should not.

Our research spans three connected layers of autonomous decision-making: risk-aware control under uncertain prediction, multi-agent coordination under information asymmetry, and integrity monitoring for AI systems. Across these settings, we develop methods that connect prediction and estimation to decision-making, control, and safety guarantees. The methods are built to travel across domains, and are validated wherever the sensing, communication, and safety constraints are well characterized enough to test them rigorously.

Research

One question, three registers

Each line of work below asks a version of the same question at a different scale: what can a system prove about a decision made under uncertainty, and what should it do when it can't prove enough.

Integrity Verification for Learned Perception

A GNSS RAIM-inspired integrity monitoring framework, built on conformal prediction to bound the error of a single learned estimate against an explicit alert limit. Grounded in ISO 26262 and ISO 21448 (SOTIF), and validated on nuScenes for 3D object localization as one instantiation of a domain-agnostic safety argument for learning-based perception in any safety-critical CPS.

In development

Multi-Agent Coordination Under Information Asymmetry

Agreement-seeking coordination for agents that must reach consensus without shared full state, currently instantiated as agreement-based cooperative driving automation: message-level protocol design (ASM/ISM/BSM), perception-accuracy evaluation, and on-track validation of full-stack decision-making under real vehicle-in-the-loop conditions.

On-track validation underway

Control and Prediction Under Uncertainty

Optimal control strategies that stay safe and efficient when the forecasts they depend on, of a leading vehicle's trajectory, a system's future state, or another agent's intent, are necessarily imperfect. This is the lab's earliest line of work and the foundation the other two build on.

Ongoing

Intersection Safety & Smart Testbeds

Infrastructure and instrumentation for intersection-scale safety research, including a proposed "mini city" smart transportation testbed: a repeatable, instrumented environment for evaluating the three lines above against real conditions rather than simulation alone.

Proposal stage
